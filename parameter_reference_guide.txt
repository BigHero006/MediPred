================================================================================
                    MediPred - CLINICAL PARAMETER REFERENCE GUIDE
================================================================================

This file provides clinical reference ranges (Low, Normal, High) for all parameters
used in the disease prediction models. Use these ranges to understand what values
are considered normal vs. abnormal.

================================================================================
DIABETES PREDICTION - 8 Parameters
================================================================================

1. PREGNANCIES
   Description: Number of times the patient has been pregnant
   Low:     0 pregnancies
   Normal:  1-3 pregnancies
   High:    4+ pregnancies
   Unit: Count

2. GLUCOSE (Fasting Blood Sugar)
   Description: Fasting blood glucose level
   Low:     70-99 mg/dL (fasting)
   Normal:  100-125 mg/dL (normal to pre-diabetic range)
   High:    126+ mg/dL (diabetic range)
   Unit: mg/dL

3. BLOOD PRESSURE (Resting)
   Description: Systolic blood pressure measurement
   Low:     <90 mmHg (hypotension)
   Normal:  90-120 mmHg (optimal to normal)
   High:    120-140+ mmHg (elevated to hypertension)
   Unit: mmHg

4. SKIN THICKNESS (Triceps)
   Description: Triceps skin fold thickness measurement
   Low:     <10 mm
   Normal:  10-30 mm
   High:    30+ mm (indicates higher body fat)
   Unit: mm

5. INSULIN (2-Hour Serum Insulin)
   Description: Insulin level after glucose tolerance test
   Low:     0-16 mU/L
   Normal:  16-166 mU/L
   High:    166+ mU/L (insulin resistance)
   Unit: mU/L

6. BMI (Body Mass Index)
   Description: Weight to height ratio (weight in kg / height in m²)
   Low:     <18.5 (underweight)
   Normal:  18.5-24.9 (healthy weight)
   High:    25-29.9 (overweight) / 30+ (obese)
   Unit: kg/m²

7. DIABETES PEDIGREE FUNCTION
   Description: Family history of diabetes (calculated score)
   Low:     0.0-0.4 (minimal genetic risk)
   Normal:  0.4-0.9 (moderate genetic risk)
   High:    0.9+ (significant genetic risk)
   Unit: Score (0-2.5)

8. AGE
   Description: Age of the patient
   Low:     <30 years (young adult)
   Normal:  30-55 years (middle age)
   High:    55+ years (senior)
   Unit: Years

================================================================================
HEART DISEASE PREDICTION - 13 Parameters
================================================================================

1. AGE
   Description: Age of the patient
   Low:     <40 years
   Normal:  40-60 years
   High:    60+ years
   Unit: Years

2. SEX
   Description: Gender of patient
   Value: 0 = Female, 1 = Male
   Unit: Binary

3. CHEST PAIN TYPE
   Description: Type of chest pain experienced
   0 = Typical angina (classic heart pain)
   1 = Atypical angina (unusual chest pain)
   2 = Non-anginal pain (not cardiac)
   3 = Asymptomatic (no chest pain)
   Unit: Category (0-3)

4. RESTING BLOOD PRESSURE
   Description: Systolic blood pressure at rest
   Low:     <90 mmHg (hypotension)
   Normal:  90-120 mmHg (optimal to normal)
   High:    140+ mmHg (hypertension)
   Unit: mmHg

5. CHOLESTEROL (Serum)
   Description: Total cholesterol level
   Low:     <200 mg/dL (desirable)
   Normal:  200-239 mg/dL (borderline high)
   High:    240+ mg/dL (high risk)
   Unit: mg/dL

6. FASTING BLOOD SUGAR
   Description: Fasting glucose >120 mg/dL
   0 = No (≤120 mg/dL - normal)
   1 = Yes (>120 mg/dL - elevated)
   Unit: Binary

7. RESTING ECG RESULTS
   Description: Electrocardiogram findings at rest
   0 = Normal
   1 = ST-T wave abnormality (possible ischemia)
   2 = LV hypertrophy (left ventricle enlarged)
   Unit: Category (0-2)

8. MAX HEART RATE ACHIEVED
   Description: Maximum heart rate during exercise test
   Low:     <60 bpm (athletic or abnormal)
   Normal:  100-140 bpm (normal for exercise)
   High:    140+ bpm (high exertion response)
   Unit: bpm (beats per minute)

9. EXERCISE INDUCED ANGINA
   Description: Chest pain triggered by exercise
   0 = No (normal response)
   1 = Yes (abnormal - ischemic response)
   Unit: Binary

10. ST DEPRESSION (Oldpeak)
    Description: ST segment depression induced by exercise relative to rest
    Low:     0.0-0.5 mm (normal)
    Normal:  0.5-1.0 mm (mild depression)
    High:    1.0+ mm (significant ischemic change)
    Unit: mm

11. ST SLOPE
    Description: Slope of ST segment during exercise
    0 = Upsloping (favorable)
    1 = Flat (neutral)
    2 = Downsloping (unfavorable/ischemic)
    Unit: Category (0-2)

12. MAJOR VESSELS (Calcification)
    Description: Number of major vessels with >50% narrowing (visible on fluoroscopy)
    Low:     0 vessels (normal)
    Normal:  0-1 vessels (mild disease)
    High:    2-3 vessels (significant disease)
    Unit: Count (0-3)

13. THALASSEMIA
    Description: Blood disorder related to abnormal hemoglobin
    0 = Normal
    1 = Fixed defect (old myocardial infarction)
    2 = Reversible defect (ischemia during stress)
    3 = Other/Unknown
    Unit: Category (0-3)

PARKINSON'S PREDICTION - 22 Voice/Vocal Metrics
================================================================================

Note: These are acoustic measurements derived from voice recordings. Most are
standardized in the Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS).

1. MDVP FO (Fundamental Frequency)
   Description: Average vocal frequency
   Low:     <100 Hz (abnormal, too low)
   Normal:  120-250 Hz (typical for adults)
   High:    250+ Hz (abnormal, too high)
   Unit: Hz

2. MDVP FHI (Maximum Fundamental Frequency)
   Description: Highest frequency reached in phonation
   Low:     <150 Hz
   Normal:  180-350 Hz
   High:    350+ Hz
   Unit: Hz

3. MDVP FLO (Minimum Fundamental Frequency)
   Description: Lowest frequency reached in phonation
   Low:     <80 Hz (very low)
   Normal:  80-150 Hz
   High:    150+ Hz
   Unit: Hz

4. MDVP JITTER (%)
   Description: Perturbation in frequency as percentage
   Low:     0.0-0.5% (healthy)
   Normal:  0.5-1.0% (mild perturbation)
   High:    1.0%+ (significant instability - Parkinsons indicator)
   Unit: Percentage

5. MDVP JITTER (Absolute)
   Description: Absolute perturbation in seconds
   Low:     0.0-0.00002 sec (healthy)
   Normal:  0.00002-0.00005 sec
   High:    0.00005+ sec (voice instability)
   Unit: Seconds

6. RAP (Relative Amplitude Perturbation)
   Description: Relative difference of amplitude in consecutive periods
   Low:     0.0-0.003 (healthy)
   Normal:  0.003-0.006 (mild perturbation)
   High:    0.006+ (significant - Parkinsons indicator)
   Unit: Ratio

7. PPQ (Pitch Period Perturbation Quotient)
   Description: Smoothed perturbation of pitch periods
   Low:     0.0-0.003 (healthy)
   Normal:  0.003-0.006
   High:    0.006+ (voice instability)
   Unit: Ratio

8. JITTER DDP (Jitter Difference of Differences)
   Description: Average absolute difference of differences between periods
   Low:     0.0-0.006 (healthy)
   Normal:  0.006-0.012
   High:    0.012+ (significant irregularity)
   Unit: Ratio

9. MDVP SHIMMER
   Description: Perturbation in amplitude (linear scale)
   Low:     0.0-0.03 (healthy)
   Normal:  0.03-0.06 (mild shimmer)
   High:    0.06+ (significant amplitude variation - Parkinsons)
   Unit: Ratio

10. MDVP SHIMMER (dB)
    Description: Perturbation in amplitude (decibel scale)
    Low:     0.0-0.3 dB (healthy)
    Normal:  0.3-0.6 dB (mild amplitude variation)
    High:    0.6+ dB (significant - Parkinsons indicator)
    Unit: dB (decibels)

11. SHIMMER APQ3
    Description: 3-period amplitude perturbation quotient
    Low:     0.0-0.015 (healthy)
    Normal:  0.015-0.03
    High:    0.03+ (amplitude instability)
    Unit: Ratio

12. SHIMMER APQ5
    Description: 5-period amplitude perturbation quotient
    Low:     0.0-0.02 (healthy)
    Normal:  0.02-0.04
    High:    0.04+ (significant instability)
    Unit: Ratio

13. MDVP APQ
    Description: Amplitude perturbation quotient
    Low:     0.0-0.02 (healthy)
    Normal:  0.02-0.04
    High:    0.04+ (irregular amplitude)
    Unit: Ratio

14. SHIMMER DDA
    Description: Difference of differences of amplitude
    Low:     0.0-0.03 (healthy)
    Normal:  0.03-0.06
    High:    0.06+ (significant variation - Parkinsons)
    Unit: Ratio

15. NHR (Noise-to-Harmonics Ratio)
    Description: Noise content relative to harmonic content
    Low:     0.0-0.05 (healthy voice)
    Normal:  0.05-0.15 (some noise)
    High:    0.15+ (high noise - Parkinsons indicator)
    Unit: Ratio

16. HNR (Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio)
    Description: Ratio of harmonic to noise components (inverse of NHR)
    Low:     <15 dB (high noise - Parkinsons)
    Normal:  15-25 dB (balanced)
    High:    25+ dB (very clear voice)
    Unit: dB

17. RPDE (Recurrence Period Density Entropy)
    Description: Complexity measure of vocal patterns
    Low:     <0.3 (simple - possible pathology)
    Normal:  0.3-0.6 (moderate complexity)
    High:    0.6+ (complex patterns - healthier)
    Unit: Entropy (0-1)

18. DFA (Detrended Fluctuation Analysis)
    Description: Self-similarity of voice signal
    Low:     <0.6 (low correlation - possible pathology)
    Normal:  0.6-0.8 (moderate correlation)
    High:    0.8+ (strong self-similarity - healthier)
    Unit: Scale exponent (0-1)

19. SPREAD1
    Description: Non-linear measure of frequency variation
    Low:     <-6 (abnormal)
    Normal:  -6 to -4 (typical range)
    High:    -4+ (abnormal - too spread)
    Unit: Score (-10 to 10)

20. SPREAD2
    Description: Non-linear measure of frequency variation (complementary)
    Low:     <0.2 (low variation)
    Normal:  0.2-0.5 (typical variation)
    High:    0.5+ (high variation)
    Unit: Score (0-1)

21. D2 (Correlation Dimension)
    Description: Complexity of voice dynamics
    Low:     <2 (low complexity - possible pathology)
    Normal:  2-3 (moderate complexity)
    High:    3+ (high complexity)
    Unit: Dimensionality (0-5)

22. PPE (Pitch Period Entropy)
    Description: Entropy of voice pitch changes
    Low:     <0.1 (very low variation - pathology)
    Normal:  0.1-0.4 (typical variation)
    High:    0.4+ (high entropy)
    Unit: Entropy (0-1)

================================================================================
HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE
================================================================================

1. For DIAGNOSIS: Compare patient's measured values against these ranges
2. For MODEL INPUT: Enter values within the specified ranges (though model can accept
                    wider ranges for research purposes)
3. For INTERPRETATION: Values marked as "High" tend to indicate elevated disease risk
4. DISCLAIMER: These ranges are clinical approximations. Always consult medical
               professionals for definitive diagnosis. ML models are supportive tools
               only and should not replace clinical judgment.

================================================================================
IMPORTANT NOTES
================================================================================

- All measurements should be taken under standardized clinical conditions
- For Parkinson's voice metrics: Use consistent audio recording settings
- Some parameters have overlap zones; clinical context is important
- Model accuracy varies by disease (Diabetes: 72%, Heart: 80%, Parkinson's: 95%)
- These are screening tools, not diagnostic instruments
